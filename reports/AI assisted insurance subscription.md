# AI drafts the policy, humans bind it

Across the world, the insurers that use AI in quote-to-bind mostly give it the preparation work: reading documents, pre-filling, checking eligibility, triaging and recommending. A licensed human still issues the quote, binds the contract and owns any adverse decision. Selling with no human involved is limited to simple, high-volume personal lines (Allstate in three US states, Ping An's AI salesforce in China), and travel and assistance players have put AI into claims and service, not into sales. The research literature backs this split. It supports automating information acquisition and analysis, recommending rather than deciding, and putting approval gates in front of binding actions, because human–AI teams on average underperform the better of the two alone on decision tasks. The aim of the design is therefore *appropriate* reliance, not maximum acceptance. Regulation points the same way. In Morocco, Law 09-08 Art. 11 bars decisions with legal effect that rest solely on automated profiling, the CNDP is openly hostile to biometric face matching, ACAPS KYC rules apply to every subscription channel, and no AI-specific ACAPS circular exists yet. The EU AI Act only reaches Africa First Assist indirectly, and is a benchmark more than a binding rule. For the IRIS four-step flow, the defensible design is "AI-oriented" automation of data capture (CIN OCR, duplicate search, consistency checks), "assisted" recommendation of offers and modules with contrastive, sourced explanations, and gates that only a human can pass at the Récapitulatif before anything is issued. I found no published case of a front-office AI copilot at any Moroccan insurer or assistance company, so Africa First Assist would be early, and its internal evidence would have to stand in for external benchmarks.

A note on evidence quality runs through this report. Many notes say page fetches failed: most figures for insurers, Morocco and regulation come from search-result summaries, not full-text reads. Several academic citations were given from the researcher's memory and their DOIs were not re-checked. Almost every industry metric is self-reported. I mark these as *(snippet)*, *(from memory)* or *(company claim)* where they matter. None of the regulatory reading here is legal advice; it needs validation by a Moroccan lawyer and Africa First Assist's DPO.

## Large insurers automate preparation, not binding

The best-documented deployments at global carriers are assistants to underwriters and contact centres. **Zurich North America's Sixfold tool covered 80% of submissions for 16 underwriters and saved about 60 minutes per submission**, and underwriters still decide ([Zurich NA](https://www.zurichna.com/media/news-releases/2025/ai-tool-brings-real-world-value-to-zurich-usmm-underwriters)). Zurich UK reports a **78% cut in commercial underwriting processing time** ([Insurance Post](https://www.postonline.co.uk/technology/7960848/zurich-cuts-commercial-underwriting-time-by-78-with-ai)). Aviva says its GP-report summariser **roughly halves underwriter review time** ([Aviva](https://www.aviva.com/newsroom/news-releases/2025/11/aviva-to-launch-groundbreaking-ai-underwriting-tool/)). Allianz UK's BRIAN answered **more than 13,000 underwriter queries and saved an estimated 135 working days** ([Allianz UK](https://www.allianz.co.uk/news-and-insight/news/brian-the-ai-brain.html)). AXA reports scale of adoption rather than outcomes: Secure GPT reached 105,000 employees, and AXA states an intent to use AI to cut agents' administrative load ([AXA Integrated Report 2023](https://www-axa-com.cdn.axa-contento-118412.eu/www-axa-com/8aa103fe-d0aa-40fb-9dd4-6f7de7712df8_axa_ri2023_accessibleb_va.pdf)). Evident's 2025 index, which ranked Allianz and AXA at the top, concluded that **"revenue-generating AI is yet to appear"** from the outside ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/06/24/insurance-companies-ai-ranking-allianz-axa-evident-insights-eye-on-ai/)). Because all these numbers are company-reported and were read from search summaries, they show what insurers choose to measure (time and effort) better than they show verified impact.

Autonomous selling exists, but only at the edges. **Ping An reports sales "realized by AI agents" of about RMB140bn in 2024 and RMB133bn in 2025, with AI handling roughly 80% of service interactions** ([Ping An 2024 deck](https://group.pingan.com/resource/pingan/IR-Docs/2025/pingan-ar24-presentation.pdf); [Ping An 2025](https://group.pingan.com/media/news/2026/ar-25.html)). Ping An does not say whether those sales were closed autonomously or merely involved AI, and the slight decline suggests the channel is not growing. Allstate's CEO reportedly said on the Q1 2026 call that its AI sells policies with no agent in three states, and called it "more of a learning". This comes from a secondary analysis that was not checked against the transcript ([actuary.info](https://actuary.info/insights/allstate-ai-direct-sales-closing-policies-distribution-2026)). Progressive's CEO is similarly reported as expecting agentic sales in the direct channel while keeping agents for complex "knee-to-knee" needs (secondary, unverified: [Perspective AI](https://getperspective.ai/blog/ai-insurance-customer-service-2026-state-of-conversational-carriers)). Tokio Marine's 2025 partnerships with OpenAI and Salesforce put agents into branches and agencies "from policy enrollment through after-sales", but published no outcomes ([Tokio Marine/Salesforce](https://www.tokiomarinehd.com/en/newsroom/release/o1ckc9000001ymth-att/20250808_Salesforce_e.pdf)). The only sales-uplift figure is anonymous: McKinsey cites an unnamed European insurer with **2–3x conversion** from AI-personalised campaigns and real-time sales coaching ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/agents-for-growth-turning-ai-promise-into-impact)). That is a consultancy claim and should not be used as a benchmark.

Travel and assistance players, the closest peers to Africa First Assist, have put AI on the claims side. **Allianz Partners US cut the average claim lifecycle from 19 days to 4**, with AI that "reviews and recommends" claims for human approval, while quoting stays a standard web form ([Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Travel-Agent-Issues/Allianz-Partners-leverages-AI-for-faster-payouts)). Allianz Partners' HCF chat channel in Australia launched with only human agents and AI "to be introduced" later ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/allianz-partners-introduces-instant-online-chat-for-hcf-travel-insurance-customers-302113945.html)). On the sales side, the innovation is embedded distribution. Chubb Studio's AI engine makes persona-matched recommendations with a "click-to-engage" hand-off to a human adviser across more than 250 partners ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/chubb-unveils-new-ai-powered-embedded-insurance-optimisation-engine/)). A third-party API catalog lists AXA Partners' travel API as callable by AI agents, which is not an AXA announcement ([Jentic](https://jentic.com/apis/axa-assistance.com/axa-assistance)). I found no Europ Assistance, AXA Assistance or Allianz Partners source on a copilot for sales agents, or any conversion metric for AI-assisted travel insurance sales.

## AI-native brokers and vendors converge on citations and confidence thresholds

The insurtech tier shows the same split, with sharper interface patterns. Lemonade is the only company with **filed** automation figures. In its FY2025 10-K, **AI Jim takes 96% of first notices of loss without human intervention and about 55% of claims are automated**, and claims AI Jim is not authorised to settle, or flags, go to humans ([Lemonade 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691421/000169142126000016/lmnd-20251231.htm)). Lemonade's FY2023 filing said AI Jim also "pays or declines" claims. The FY2024–2025 filings drop that language ([10-K FY2023](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691421/000169142124000026/lmnd-20231231.htm)). Reading that as a retreat from implied automated denials is an inference, not something Lemonade says, but it matches where the litigation risk lies. Lemonade's 2021 "non-verbal cues" episode ended in a **$4M Illinois BIPA settlement** over biometric data taken from claim videos without consent ([Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/closed-settlements/lemonade-insurance-biometric-privacy-4m-class-action-settlement/)). That is directly relevant to any idea of adding face matching to CIN capture.

AI-native brokers raised very large rounds in 2025–2026 but describe their human–AI split only in marketing terms. Harper, a licensed commercial brokerage, raised about $47M. It says AI reads applications, routes submissions and chases underwriters, while account managers stay in the loop, and it claims coverage in **24–48 hours instead of weeks** *(company claim)* ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/02/26/harper-raises-47m-to-modernise-business-insurance-with-ai/)). Corgi went from carrier approval in July 2025 to a reported $4B valuation ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/140973-corgi-raises-again-at-4b-valuation-its-third-round-since-may/)). None of these firms publishes bind rates, loss ratios or error rates. Tools sold to existing agencies are getting cheap: bolttech's "bolt AI for Agencies" answers calls, takes intake and produces "real-time bindable quotes" without published figures ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2025/10/21/bolt-ai-for-agencies-transforms-insurance-quoting-process/)), and white-label voice agents start at $99/month ([Trillet](https://trillet.ai/blogs/voice-agent-for-insurance-agencies)). Indemn's "Agent Copilot", which suggests replies to human agents during live chats, is the closest commercial analogue to what IRIS would do ([Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/east/2024/02/01/758902.htm)). Alan's LLM claim agent fully resolved **about 30% of the claims-related support tickets** it received in its first release, which is a sensible ceiling for what LLM service agents close without a human ([Alan blog, snippet only](https://medium.com/alan/inside-alans-claim-agent-how-an-ai-investigates-claims-just-like-a-human-only-faster-11d62bd7efc9)).

The interface pattern for professionals is the most useful finding for a UX designer. Underwriting vendors converge on four elements: structured extraction from messy inputs, a ranked triage queue, a recommendation with source citations, and insurer-configurable confidence thresholds that decide when a human must review. Sixfold links each finding to the source document and the relevant manual provision, and lets insurers set how much review is required ([Insurance Edge](https://insurance-edge.net/2026/09/03/sixfold-launches-ai-underwriter-for-life-health/)). Cytora sends cases to a human according to per-flow confidence thresholds ([Cytora](https://cytora.com/configurable-risk-flows)). In every vendor found, final bind authority stays with a human. I found no independent test of citation accuracy, hallucination rates or underwriter override rates for any of them.

## Morocco's agent channel is large, under pressure and untouched by AI copilots

Front-office tooling targets the channel that carries Moroccan non-life business. The 2024 market was about **MAD 63.1bn with penetration of 3.7%**. Bancassurance held 31.9% of the market and brokers 30.4%, so agents-general and direct offices hold roughly 38% by subtraction. Because 96% of bancassurance is life and savings, non-life lines such as assistance and travel run mainly through agents, brokers and direct offices *(snippet)* ([Atlas Magazine](https://atlas-mag.net/fr/articles/marche-marocain-de-la-bancassurance-en-2024)). Africa First Assist, formerly Saham Assistance and part of Sanlam, ranked **third in Moroccan assistance in 2024 at about MAD 401.7m**, behind Maroc Assistance Internationale (MAD 588.8m) and Wafa IMA (MAD 428.4m) *(snippet)* ([Atlas Mag ranking 2024](https://atlas-mag.net/fr/articles/classement-des-assureurs-marocains-selon-le-chiffre-daffaires-2024)). In 2016 it led the segment with 38.8% ([FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-financiere-maroc/les-pdm-du-marche-de-l-assistance-au-maroc)). That loss of relative share is a business reason to invest in front-office productivity. Intermediary counts conflict between sources (603 brokers and 1,859 agents in March 2026 per [Challenge.ma](https://www.challenge.ma/le-paradoxe-du-courtage-marocain-indispensable-mais-sous-estime-324638/), versus 2,154 intermediaries per a Hespress snippet). The trade press repeatedly describes the network as "under pressure" and afraid of digital disintermediation ([FNH](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualite-financiere-maroc/distribution-digitale-la-peur-bleue-des-intermediaires-en-assurance)). ACAPS has sanctioned 167 intermediaries and withdrawn two licences ([Infomédiaire](https://www.infomediaire.net/?p=127469)), so compliance guardrails built into the tool have value of their own.

Travel demand is document-heavy and driven by deadlines. Moroccans filed about **606,800 Schengen applications in 2024 with a refusal rate of about 20%** ([HelloSafe](https://hellosafe.com/fr/schengen-visa/from-morocco)). The visa requires travel medical cover of at least €30k for care, hospitalisation and repatriation (commercial source: [HelloSafe](https://hellosafe.com/fr/schengen-visa/travel-insurance/from-morocco)), and the Spanish consulate in Rabat requires valid cover on each entry for multiple-entry visas ([Exteriores.gob.es](https://exteriores.gob.es/DocumentosAuxiliaresSC/Marruecos/RABAT%20(C)/Requisitos%20visados/FRA/1.VISA%20SCHENGEN%20POUR%20TOURISME%20%20VISITE%20À%20LA%20FAMILLE%20%20AFFAIRES.pdf)). A 2023 consumer complaint said insurers do not refund travel cover after a visa refusal *(snippet)*, which makes refund and cancellation terms a point where the customer's trust breaks. Hajj and Umrah form a second pillar sold through travel agencies. FNAVM's optional Hajj cancellation product with Isaaf Mondial Assistance (Isaaf Mondial is an Africa First Assist product, confirmed by the user; IRIS is its front- and back-office portal) is priced at 2% of the package *(snippet)* ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/441379-hajj-et-omra-mesures-renforcees-pour-proteger-les-pelerins-marocains.html)), so a B2B2C path matters too.

Moroccan AI initiatives are aimed at customers. AtlantaSanad's "Assia" (Sept 2025) is billed as the country's first insurance AI assistant ([Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/atlantasanad-devoile-assia-premiere-assistante-ia-dediee-a-lassurance/302166)). La Marocaine Vie won a 2025 award for the first fully online subscription with no break in the journey ([Financial Afrik](https://www.financialafrik.com/2025/02/18/les-laureats-des-trophees-de-lassurance-dafrique-2025-au-maroc-un-tournant-vers-linnovation/)). ACAPS runs an insurtech programme with the AfDB to 2028 ([Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/le-maroc-mobilise-lecosysteme-insurtech-pour-une-assurance-accessible/337522)), yet online sales still lagged "despite a complete regulatory framework" in 2023 ([Médias24](https://medias24.com/2023/06/06/assurances-la-vente-en-ligne-a-la-traine-malgre-un-cadre-reglementaire-complet/)). I found no Moroccan insurer publicly using CIN OCR or an agent copilot. A tool that makes agents stronger, rather than going around them, fits a network that fears disintermediation. WhatsApp is the dominant channel, used by **75% of Moroccans, 95% of them daily** ([TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2025/05/30/reseaux-sociaux-au-maroc-whatsapp-facebook-et-instagram-dominent-19-des-marocains-toujours-hors-ligne_1935598/)), which makes it the obvious way to deliver certificates and summaries. Law 43-20 sets three e-signature levels, and only the qualified level is fully equivalent to a handwritten signature ([SNRT News](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/signature-en-ligne-au-maroc-entre-securite-technique-absolue-et-parfaite-equivalence-legale)). I found no published data on the language mix (French, Arabic, Darija, Amazigh) in insurance interactions, so French-first with Arabic support is an assumption, not a finding.

## The science says automate the inputs, recommend the choice, gate the action

The human-factors literature gives a precise frame for the three levels (supported, assisted, AI-oriented). Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens split automation into four stages: information acquisition, information analysis, decision selection and action implementation. Each stage gets its own level. High automation of decisions is reserved for cases where reliability is high and the consequences of error are low ([Parasuraman et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354), *from memory*). Sheridan's level 5, "executes if the human approves", is an approval checkpoint. Level 6, a limited window to veto, suits only low-consequence, reversible steps. Onnasch et al.'s meta-analysis found that higher automation improves routine performance but makes failures much worse when the automation fails: **the higher the automation, the harder the fall** ([Onnasch et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720813501549), *from memory*). Endsley's "automation conundrum" adds that the more reliable a system becomes, the less likely its supervisor is to catch its rare failures ([Endsley 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720816681350), *from memory*). Bainbridge's "ironies of automation" warns that automating routine work leaves people with the hardest exceptions while taking away the practice they need to handle them ([Bainbridge 1983](https://doi.org/10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8), *from memory*). Recent agent frameworks restate this as the user's role, from operator through collaborator, consultant and approver to observer ([Feng et al. 2025, preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12469)), and as concrete mechanisms: co-planning, and "action guards" before irreversible steps with user-configurable frequency ([Magentic-UI, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22358)). None of these studies was run in insurance distribution, so applying them to IRIS is by analogy.

The central empirical warning is that pairing a person with an AI does not by itself improve decisions. **Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone's meta-analysis of 106 experiments found that human–AI combinations performed worse than the best of human or AI alone (Hedges' g = −0.23)**. The losses were concentrated in decision tasks and the gains in content creation. Combining them helped only when the human was better than the AI ([Vaccaro et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11659167/)). People both over-rely and under-rely. Automation bias produces omission and commission errors in experts as well as novices ([Parasuraman & Manzey 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720810376055), *from memory*), yet people abandon algorithms faster than humans after seeing them err ([Dietvorst et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000033), *from memory*). One result is directly usable: **letting people modify algorithmic outputs, even slightly, greatly increases their use** ([Dietvorst et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2643), *from memory*). Cognitive forcing (deciding before seeing the AI, slowing down, AI on demand) cuts over-reliance, but users rate those designs least favourably ([Buçinca et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287), *from memory*). That argues for using forcing only at high-stakes moments. Fügener et al. found that **AI delegating uncertain cases to humans beats humans deciding when to delegate**, because people are poor at knowing when they need help ([Fügener et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.25300/MISQ/2021/16553), *from memory*). Green's review is the strongest counter-position, and it deserves to be taken seriously. "Human in the loop" policies often fail because overseers cannot reliably detect errors, so a validation click can legitimise a flawed system unless oversight is shown to work in practice ([Green 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clsr.2022.105681), *from memory*). The metric that follows comes from Schemmer et al.: measure **appropriate reliance**, meaning switching to the AI when it is right and keeping one's own answer when it is wrong, not acceptance rate ([Schemmer et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3581641.3584066), *from memory*).

Explanations help only when they make checking cheaper. Bansal et al. found explanations increased acceptance of AI advice **whether or not it was correct** ([Bansal et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445717), *from memory*). Vasconcelos et al. showed that explanations reduce over-reliance when they cut the cost of verifying the AI relative to doing the task ([Vasconcelos et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.06823)). Zhang, Liao and Bellamy found that confidence scores calibrated trust where local feature explanations did not ([Zhang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.02114)). Miller's synthesis says useful explanations are contrastive ("why this rather than that"), selective and social ([Miller 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2018.07.007), *from memory*). Liao et al.'s XAI question bank (why, why not, what if, what data, how reliable) gives a ready template for explanation UI ([Liao et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376590), *from memory*). The closest study to insurance sales is Ehsan et al.'s **social transparency** work, in which showing colleagues' past decisions on similar cases helped sellers in an enterprise pricing scenario calibrate trust ([Ehsan et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445188), *from memory*). For LLM text specifically, first-person hedges ("I'm not sure, but…") reduced over-reliance on wrong answers ([Kim et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3630106.3658941), *from memory*), and longer, fluent explanations raised user confidence without raising accuracy ([Steyvers et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00976-7), *from memory*).

Field evidence on generative AI at work is positive for productivity and skewed toward novices. In the largest study, a GPT-based assistant for about 5,000 support agents raised **issues resolved per hour by about 15% on average and about 34% for novices**, with little effect on the most experienced ([Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771v1)). The BCG "jagged frontier" experiment found large gains inside the AI's competence. On a task just outside it, consultants using AI were reportedly **19 points less likely to be correct** (figures from memory of the working paper; [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4573321)). A retail field experiment across the sales funnel found effects from 0 to 16.3%, mainly through conversion, depending on how much each tool added to existing practice ([Fang et al., working paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12049)). Unrestricted GPT-4 access improved students' practice scores but lowered their later unaided performance, while a guarded tutor mode reduced the harm ([Bastani et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), *from memory*). That matters for training new agents. Amershi et al.'s 18 guidelines remain the checklist, especially G2 (say how well it works), G8–G9 (efficient dismissal and correction), G10 (scope down when in doubt) and G16 (convey consequences) ([Amershi et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233), *from memory*). Kellogg et al.'s finding that algorithmic control of workers provokes "algoactivism" ([Kellogg et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.5465/annals.2018.0174), *from memory*) is a reminder that override logs can feel like surveillance. The State Farm case below shows the same thing in practice.

The academic work specific to insurance is strong on pricing fairness and weak on human–AI interaction. Lindholm et al. formalise direct versus indirect (proxy) discrimination and give a discrimination-free pricing method ([Lindholm et al. 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/ED25C4053690E56050F437B8DF2AD117/S0515036121000234a.pdf/div-class-title-discrimination-free-insurance-pricing-div.pdf)). That is a strong argument for keeping any LLM out of rating. In robo-advice, D'Acunto et al. found real benefits for less sophisticated investors and pitfalls for sophisticated ones ([D'Acunto et al. 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v32y2019i5p1983-2020..html)). Filiz et al. found people delegated to a clearly superior robo-advisor **only about 40% of the time** ([Filiz et al. 2022](https://publications.goettingen-research-online.de/ID-117073)). A 2026 preprint on agentic commercial underwriting found that an adversarial critic agent reduced hallucinations across 500 cases while humans kept binding authority (snippet only; [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.13213)). The gap is plain: I found **no PhD thesis on AI assistants in insurance distribution and no peer-reviewed field study of GenAI copilots used by insurance agents during a sale**. theses.fr was not searchable from the research environment, so a direct theses.fr or ProQuest search is still outstanding.

## Every regime puts the binding decision on a human

Morocco has no AI law and no ACAPS AI circular. A March 2026 secondary source confirms none had been published ([212assurances](https://www.212assurances.com/2026/03/16/le-maroc-renforce-ses-competences-en-ia-et-cybersecurite-pour-lassurance/)). The binding constraints are existing law. In Law 09-08 (read from prior knowledge of the text, as [cndp.ma](https://www.cndp.ma/fr/loi-09-08/) was unreachable), Art. 11 forbids decisions with legal effect based solely on automated profiling. Art. 1 and Art. 21 treat health data as sensitive and require authorisation to process it, to use the national ID number for other purposes, or to interconnect files. Art. 5 sets information duties at collection. Art. 43–44 restrict transfers to non-adequate countries, which matters for US-hosted LLM or OCR APIs. Secondary sources add that remote access from abroad can itself count as a transfer and that vendor standard clauses may need a 09-08 addendum ([9anonai](https://9anonai.com/fr/blog/cndp-prior-authorization-data-transfer-2026), unverified). The CNDP moved from awareness to enforcement in February 2025 ([Lafrouji Avocats](https://lafroujiavocats.com/protection-donnees-maroc-cndp-maroc/)), and its August 2026 rules for political parties asked for AI-generated content to be flagged ([H24info](https://h24info.ma/maroc/politique/legislatives-2026-donnees-personnelles-ia-la-cndp-fixe-les-regles/)). On biometrics, the CNDP imposed a facial-recognition moratorium in 2019. Its framework deliberation D-195-EUS/2020 expresses **strong reservations about providers building their own biometric databases, "sometimes hosted outside national territory"**, and favours a national trusted third party ([CNDP D-195](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/CNDP-Dlibration_D-195-EUS-2020-Usage_de_la_reconnaissance_faciale-_20210421.pdf)). Plain OCR of a CIN is ordinary personal-data processing; adding face matching or liveness checks would bring in the biometric regime.

The insurance code adds KYC and duty-to-advise obligations. Law 17-99's Book IV governs intermediaries ([ACAPS consolidated Code](https://www.acaps.ma/sites/default/files/2025-02/Loi_17-99_Code%20des%20assurances%20consolid%C3%A9_FR.pdf)). ACAPS AML Circular AS/02/19, Art. 20, subjects remote and electronic subscription to the **same identification and verification conditions as face-to-face** ([ACAPS AS/02/19](https://www.acaps.ma/sites/default/files/circulaire_as-02-19_fr.pdf)). A reformed Book IV that strengthens the duty to advise has been filed with the SGG and targets publication in 2027 ([EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/entretien-abderrahim-chaffai-acaps-vise-le-cap-2027-pour-le-nouveau-livre-iv/)). ACAPS also plans **AI-based monitoring of online sales and communications** ([Industrie du Maroc](https://industries.ma/lacaps-lance-le-programme-emergence-pour-accelerer-la-transformation-numerique-des-assurances/)), so AI-generated scripts and documents should be expected to come under scrutiny. The draft "Digital X.0" law on AI, data governance and digital identity is under SGG review ([Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-digital/0511-50160-morocco-advances-with-digital-x-0-law-to-regulate-artificial-intelligence)). ACAPS is an IAIS member, so the IAIS's July 2025 AI application paper (governance, explainability, fairness, redress) is the closest international benchmark ([IAIS](https://www.iais.org/2025/07/the-iais-publishes-application-paper-on-the-supervision-of-artificial-intelligence/)).

Foreign regimes matter mostly as templates. The EU AI Act lists only **AI for risk assessment and pricing of natural persons in life and health insurance** as high-risk (Annex III 5(c)). The Digital Omnibus moved those obligations to **2 December 2027**, while Art. 50 transparency (telling people they are dealing with AI) has applied since 2 August 2026 ([ActuIA](https://www.actuia.com/en/news/ai-act-obligations-in-force-and-delays-under-omnibus-2026-1744/)). Art. 14 explicitly asks overseers to be aware of automation bias (from prior knowledge of the text; [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)). Two points need legal validation: whether travel *medical* cover counts as "health insurance" if AI ever prices individuals, and whether the Act's extraterritorial reach (output used in the Union) applies to policies bought for Schengen travel. Keeping pricing deterministic and outside the AI removes the first problem. EIOPA's August 2025 Opinion holds that IDD Arts. 17, 20 and 25 already govern AI. An AI recommendation is the distributor's advice, so it must meet the demands-and-needs test, stay within the product's target market and serve the customer's best interest ([EIOPA](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/eiopa-publishes-opinion-ai-governance-and-risk-management-2025-08-06_en)). The French ACPR's remote-advice practice recommends alerting on inconsistent or incomplete answers ([ACPR](https://acpr.banque-france.fr/system/files/import/acpr/medias/documents/20161123_cp_acpr_annexe_reco_conseiladistance.pdf)). The US NAIC Model Bulletin (December 2023, adopted by roughly 24–25 states plus DC depending on the source) requires a written AI programme covering inventory, risk rating, vendor oversight and documentation, across marketing and sales as well as underwriting ([NAIC map](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/legal-adoption-map-ai-model-bulletin.pdf)). That structure makes a ready template for Africa First Assist's internal AI governance file.

## Documented failures cluster in automated denials, labour and chatbot misstatements

There is no documented regulator sanction for AI-driven insurance *mis-selling*, and no documented rollback of an insurer's sales copilot. The failures that are documented fall into three patterns. The first is **automated adverse decisions with only nominal human review**. GEICO settled with the Pennsylvania AG after an AI tool flagged a customer and an auto policy was cancelled ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/geico-ai-settlement-insurance-underwriting-compliance/)). The Cigna PxDx class action was allowed to proceed in part ([Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/judge-advances-class-claims-over-cigna-use-of-automated-algorithm-to-deny-benefits/)), and UnitedHealth's nH Predict case reached discovery in 2026 ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/daraabasiita/2026/06/09/the-algorithm-that-counted-on-no-one-appealing/)). The second is **liability for what the bot says**. In Moffatt v. Air Canada the airline was held liable for its chatbot's invented bereavement-fare policy, and the tribunal rejected the argument that the bot was a separate entity ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/chatbot-tracker/2026/courts-tell-companies-they-own-what-their-chatbot-says/)). The same reasoning would apply to an AI-generated coverage summary that misstates an exclusion. The UK FCA has asked for disclaimers on AI financial advice ([Finextra](https://finextra.com/newsarticle/48604/fca-calls-for-disclaimers-on-ai-financial-advice)), and Lloyd's-market cover for chatbot errors now exists ([Armilla](https://www.armilla.ai/resources/insurers-launch-cover-for-losses-caused-by-ai-chatbot-errors)). The third is **labour backlash**. State Farm's 2026 agent contracts mandated daily AI use and tied pay to sales targets. After agents protested, State Farm slowed some of the changes in June 2026 ([WGLT](https://www.wglt.org/local-news/2026-06-26/state-farm-slows-changes-for-agent-contracts)). Reports of 35–40% income drops for some agents are unconfirmed by State Farm. For Africa First Assist, the lesson is that how AI use is framed and linked to evaluation determines adoption as much as the interface does.

## Design implications for the IRIS subscription flow

Taken together, the evidence suggests mapping the three levels to Parasuraman's stages, not to the whole flow. Each IRIS step then sits at a different level, and "AI-oriented" never means the AI binds. The table sets out the recommended level for each step. It is a synthesis from the sources above, not a tested design.

| IRIS step | Main automation stage | Recommended level | What the AI does | What the agent must do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Voyage | Acquisition and analysis | AI-oriented for capture, assisted for interpretation | Parses destination/dates; flags Schengen/Hajj requirements (e.g. €30k minimum, per-entry validity); checks date consistency | Confirms trip purpose and dates with the customer |
| 2. Offre & modularité | Decision selection | Assisted (suggest, never pre-select silently) | Proposes 2–3 options with contrastive "why this, not that"; checks target-market fit; shows the effect of adding or removing modules | Chooses the offer; records the customer's stated needs; can edit any suggestion |
| 3. Souscripteur & assurés | Acquisition (OCR, duplicates) | AI-oriented with field-level confidence | CIN OCR pre-fill with low-confidence highlighting; duplicate candidates with match reasons; consistency alerts | Verifies the identity document against the person; resolves duplicates (never auto-merged) |
| 4. Récapitulatif | Action implementation | Supported (human-only gate) | Generates a plain-language summary of guarantees, exclusions and refund terms; lists unresolved flags | Explicitly confirms disclosures and issues the policy; no veto-window auto-issue |

The priorities below follow from that mapping, ordered by regulatory exposure first and adoption value second.

| Priority | Design implication | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Pricing, eligibility and premium come only from the approved tariff engine. The LLM may explain a price but never compute or alter it. | Lindholm et al. on proxy discrimination; EU AI Act Annex III 5(c); Law 09-08 Art. 11 |
| P0 | A human gate at the Récapitulatif for issue, signature and payment, with a clear consequence statement ("this issues a binding contract for X MAD"). No auto-issue, even at the "AI-oriented" level. | Sheridan level 5; Amershi G16; Magentic-UI action guards; Law 09-08 Art. 11 |

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the request. The question is not how much of the subscription the AI should do, but **at which stage the AI may act and where the agent must think**. The external evidence supports AI-oriented automation of the inputs, assisted recommendation of the choice, and a human-only gate on the action, and Moroccan law already turns that last gate into a requirement rather than a preference. The less obvious finding is that a validation step alone protects neither the customer nor Africa First Assist. Meta-analytic evidence shows that people paired with AI often decide worse than the better of the two alone, and Green's critique shows that nominal human oversight can make flaws look legitimate. The flow's real safeguards are therefore field-level provenance, contrastive explanations that make checking quick, deterministic pricing and a measurement plan that tracks appropriate reliance.

Africa First Assist also has an unusual position. It is third in its market, has lost ground since 2016, faces no AI-specific regulation yet, and sells a product whose main pain points (Schengen deadlines, ID capture, refund disputes) suit narrow, well-bounded AI. No Moroccan peer and no academic study has shown how an agent copilot performs in insurance sales. The first well-instrumented IRIS pilot would therefore produce the evidence the literature lacks, and the logs it keeps can also serve as compliance evidence when ACAPS's Book IV reform and the Digital X.0 law arrive.
