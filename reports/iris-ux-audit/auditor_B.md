# IRIS — Independent design and UX-copy audit (Auditor B)

Scope: 31 production screens. S01–S14 cover subscription, F01–F11 the contract record ("fiche contrat") and A01–A06 the other back-office pages.
Sources: six screens were rendered with the app's real CSS (S01, S03, S04, S07, F08, A06). For all 31 screens I used the captured HTML and the visible-text extraction. Each finding is tagged **[R]** (seen on a rendered screen) or **[H]** (from HTML or text only). Icons are missing from the renders because the icon font could not load in the sandbox, so missing icons are not reported. Content of hidden drawers (product catalogue, guarantee panel, phone country list) counts as off-screen unless the HTML shows the drawer open; findings about those drawers say so.

## Overall verdict

IRIS has a solid, modern base. It uses one consistent blue system and card-based layouts, and the subscription flow has a clear 4-step stepper with a helpful context panel ("Bon à savoir", "À préparer"). The back-office pages share one pattern: KPI strip, search, advanced filters, table. Several patterns are already mature: the "Prime à recalculer" stale-price guard, the pagination summary ("1 – 10 sur 19") and well-written empty states. The serious problems are not visual polish. They are about meaning and data safety:
- The core role words of the contract are used inconsistently, and in one place contradict each other ("souscripteur", "assuré principal", "bénéficiaires").
- The price of options and upgrades is hidden at the moment the agent chooses them.
- Defaults are pre-filled on identity data (Genre = "Homme").
- The promise to "enregistrer et reprendre" a subscription is not backed by any visible save control.

Accessibility is the weakest area:
- The main navigation is a rail of unlabelled `<li>` icons with no links.
- The home product cards are not focusable.
- Several identity fields have no programmatic label, or the wrong one (duplicate IDs).
- The page language is declared as English.
- Secondary text often falls below 4.5:1 contrast.

The copy is mostly clean French, but it drifts between synonyms on almost every key concept (objet/motif, statut civil/situation maritale, type d'identité/type d'identifiant, police/contrat, branche/catégorie). It also slips into "tu", English formatting and internal jargon. For agents who repeat this flow dozens of times a day, the most valuable fixes are terminology, price transparency and keyboard efficiency.

## Fix first (top 5)

1. **B-01: Clarify souscripteur vs assuré principal.** This is the only issue that can produce a legally wrong contract, and the copy fix is cheap. The structural fix (two blocks when "Non") removes the ambiguity entirely.
2. **B-02 and B-03: Price transparency.** Show the premium impact on every option and level, and say whether the premium is HT or TTC. Agents quote these numbers to clients many times a day, so a mismatch creates disputes and slows every sale.
3. **B-05, B-06 and B-07: Make the identity form safe and accessible.** Remove the default gender, use persistent labels with optional fields marked, give each field a unique id and a label, and add autocomplete. This is the most-typed form in the product: it directly affects data quality and speed.
4. **B-08 and B-09: Make navigation and the home cards real links or buttons with names.** Today keyboard users cannot reach any section or start a subscription. This is a blocking WCAG failure and also a learnability gain for new agents (visible labels).
5. **B-04: Show a save mechanism in the subscription flow.** The home page promises it. Without a visible "Enregistrer la proposition" or an autosave status, interrupted counter conversations risk losing data and agents re-enter work.

(Next in line: B-15 on the record's three navigation layers, and B-12/B-13 on terminology consistency.)

## What works well and should be kept

- **The step-1 context panel** ("Bon à savoir" / "À préparer", S03): it tells the agent what to collect before starting, which is excellent for counter work.
- **The stale-premium guard** (S12): "Prime à recalculer – Couverture modifiée : ce montant n'en tient pas encore compte. Recalculez la prime pour continuer." It blocks "Suivant" and swaps in "Recalculer la prime". Keep the pattern and only reduce the visual weight of the stale figure (B-41).
- **The modification tracking on guarantees** (S09): a "1 modifiée" counter plus "Réinitialiser" and "Voir le plafond / l'option de base" gives clear system status and an easy undo.
- **The empty states with explanation** (A02: "Aucun contrat à échéance – Les contrats arrivant à échéance sur la période sélectionnée apparaîtront ici."; F07/A05: "Vous êtes à jour : aucune demande n'attend de réponse de votre part.").
- **The A04 error model**: "Impossible de charger les utilisateurs. Veuillez réessayer." with a "Réessayer" button. Use it as the template for all errors.
- **The consistent back-office list pattern** (A01–A06): title and subtitle, KPI strip, search, "Filtres avancés", table, and a pagination summary ("1 – 10 sur 19"). The KPI arithmetic is coherent (A06: 9 285 + 4 325 − 0 = 13 610 MAD).
- **The contract header** (F08): status badge "Actif", period with an arrow (10-11-2026 → 09-11-2028), premium, renewal and subscriber all visible above the fold. The avenants list shows each premium, and they add up to the total (3 640 + 0 + 2 750 = 6 390 MAD).
- **Accessible names already present** on the table row actions (A06 `aria-label="Détails"`), on the drawer close (`aria-label="Fermer"`) and on the tour button. Extend this practice to the rest.
- **CTAs that already start with a verb** and should stay: "Voir les offres", "Appliquer une remise", "Exporter en Excel", "Recalculer la prime", "Télécharger les conditions générales", "Reprendre / Traiter / Consulter".
- **The visual system**: one primary blue, generous card spacing, a clear primary/secondary button pair in the footer ("Précédent" / "Suivant") and a 4-step stepper that keeps the flow short.
