## 1. EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689): high-risk insurance uses, Art. 50 transparency, Art. 4 AI literacy, timelines, relevance for non-EU firms

### Takeaway
Under the AI Act, the only insurance use listed as high-risk is AI for risk assessment and pricing of natural persons in life and health insurance (Annex III 5(c)). The Digital Omnibus (Reg. 2026/1744) moved Annex III obligations to 2 December 2027. Art. 50 chatbot and generative-AI transparency has applied since 2 August 2026, and AI literacy since 2 February 2025. A travel/assistance subscription assistant used by agents is probably not high-risk. It can come close if it prices or assesses health risk, for example travel medical cover. The Act only binds a Moroccan firm if it places systems or outputs on the EU market.

### Cited Findings
- Annex III point 5(c) lists as high-risk "AI systems intended to be used for risk assessment and pricing in relation to natural persons in the case of life and health insurance". Art. 50 requires that people be informed they are interacting with an AI system, and that synthetic content be marked. Art. 4 requires providers and deployers to take measures to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy among staff. Art. 2(1)(c) applies the Act to providers and deployers in third countries where the output of the system is used in the Union. [primary text, from prior knowledge] — [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- EIOPA's summary confirms that the AI Act treats AI used for risk assessment and pricing in life and health insurance as high-risk, and that the EIOPA Opinion excludes high-risk or prohibited systems from its scope — [EIOPA press release, 6 Aug 2025](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/eiopa-publishes-opinion-ai-governance-and-risk-management-2025-08-06_en)
- The Digital Omnibus on AI is Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. It was published in the OJ on 24 July 2026 and entered into force on 27 July 2026. The Annex III high-risk deadline moved from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027, and Annex I (product-embedded) systems moved to 2 Aug 2028. The co-legislators chose fixed dates over the Commission's proposal to link them to standards being available — [ActuIA](https://www.actuia.com/en/news/ai-act-obligations-in-force-and-delays-under-omnibus-2026-1744/); [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Orrick, July 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes)
- Art. 50 transparency duties were left unchanged and have applied since 2 August 2026. Prohibitions, AI literacy and transparency duties are in operation — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [ISMS Lite](https://www.ismslite.de/en/blog/ai-act-digital-omnibus-deadlines); [Alpacax](https://www.alpacax.com/blog/eu-ai-act-high-risk-deadline-moved-to-2027-what-didnt/)
- Original timeline from the Act: prohibitions and Art. 4 AI literacy from 2 Feb 2025; GPAI obligations from 2 Aug 2025; general application 2 Aug 2026. [primary text, from prior knowledge] — [Reg. 2024/1689 Art. 113](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- Some secondary sources say the Omnibus also changed the AI-literacy duty, for example by shifting the emphasis to member states and the Commission encouraging literacy, and changed labelling duties. The exact final wording was not verified — [ISMS Lite](https://www.ismslite.de/en/blog/ai-act-digital-omnibus-deadlines); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes)

### Inferences
- A front-office assistant for travel and assistance subscription (product suggestion, form pre-fill, OCR of IDs, script generation) is not Annex III 5(c) unless it does individual risk assessment or pricing for health or medical cover. Travel medical expenses cover could arguably count as "health insurance" if the AI prices or underwrites individuals, for example on pre-existing conditions. Keeping pricing deterministic and tariff-based, outside the AI, avoids the high-risk classification. This needs legal validation.
- Art. 50(1) covers systems that interact directly with natural persons. An agent-facing copilot whose output the human agent relays falls outside that core duty, but the duty applies if the customer chats with the AI directly (web, WhatsApp). Making AI involvement visible to the customer is good practice in any case.
- For a Moroccan company selling to Moroccan residents, the AI Act does not apply directly. It becomes relevant if the company sells to EU residents (for example, Schengen-visa travel insurance bought from the EU), uses an EU-based AI provider, or partners with EU insurers or reinsurers that impose AI Act-aligned contract clauses. Its main role is as a benchmark that Moroccan regulators drafting the "Digital X.0" law are likely to look to (see section 4).

### Gaps
- Exact text of the Omnibus changes to Art. 4 (AI literacy) and the Art. 50(2) watermarking grace period: Orrick and EUR-Lex could not be fetched.
- Whether Art. 50 obligations for deployers (deepfake and text disclosure) were amended: not verified.

## 2. EIOPA: 2025 AI Opinion, 2021 digital ethics principles, IDD demands-and-needs / advice, POG, and AI-generated recommendations

### Takeaway
EIOPA's position is that existing insurance law already governs AI. IDD Arts. 17, 20 and 25, Solvency II governance and DORA apply proportionately, covering data governance, record-keeping, fairness, cybersecurity, explainability and human oversight. Under the IDD, an AI-generated recommendation is still the distributor's advice. It must satisfy the demands-and-needs test, fall within the product's target market, and act in the customer's best interest.

### Cited Findings
- EIOPA published the Opinion on AI governance and risk management on 6 Aug 2025 (EIOPA-BoS-25-360). It is addressed to national competent authorities and takes a risk-based, proportionate approach. Its legal bases are IDD Arts. 17 (act honestly, fairly, professionally, in the best interests of customers), 20 (demands and needs, information) and 25 (product oversight and governance), Solvency II Arts. 41, 46 and 82, and DORA Arts. 4–6, 11 and 12. It covers data governance, record-keeping, fairness, cybersecurity, explainability and human oversight. AI Act high-risk and prohibited systems are excluded from its scope. EIOPA plans further analyses of specific AI systems — [EIOPA press release](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/eiopa-publishes-opinion-ai-governance-and-risk-management-2025-08-06_en); [Opinion PDF](https://www.dirittobancario.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Opinion-on-Artificial-Intelligence-governance-and-risk-management.pdf); [EIOPA publication page](https://eiopa.europa.eu/publications/opinion-artificial-intelligence-governance-and-risk-management_en)
- In June 2021 EIOPA's Consultative Expert Group on Digital Ethics published "AI Governance Principles: towards ethical and trustworthy AI in the European insurance sector". The six principles are proportionality, fairness and non-discrimination, transparency and explainability, human oversight, data governance and record keeping, and robustness and performance. [primary text, from prior knowledge] — [EIOPA](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/publications/artificial-intelligence-governance-principles-towards-ethical-and-trustworthy-artificial_en)
- Under IDD (Directive (EU) 2016/97) Art. 20(1), before a contract is concluded the distributor must specify the customer's demands and needs on the basis of information obtained from the customer. Any contract proposed must be consistent with those demands and needs. Where advice is given, a personalised recommendation must explain why a particular product best meets them. Art. 20(5) and (8) require an insurance product information document (IPID) for non-life products. Art. 25 requires that products are distributed only within their identified target market. [primary text, from prior knowledge] — [IDD, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2016/97/oj)
- The French ACPR (comparable supervisory practice) treats providing information on contracts according to criteria the customer chooses on a website, where the customer can conclude the contract, as insurance distribution. For remote collection of information, it recommends handling inconsistent or incomplete answers by alerting the customer or using automatic explanation mechanisms. Its Recommendation 2024-R-02, which strengthens the duty to advise before and after subscription, has applied since 31 Dec 2025 — [Club Patrimoine](https://www.clubpatrimoine.com/contenus/devoir-conseil-assurance); [ACPR remote-advice recommendation annex](https://acpr.banque-france.fr/system/files/import/acpr/medias/documents/20161123_cp_acpr_annexe_reco_conseiladistance.pdf); [Orisha](https://insurance.orisha.com/blog/professionnel-assurance/dda-assurance/)

### Inferences
- Requirements for an AI-assisted subscription flow, inferred from the Opinion, the IDD and the 2021 principles:
  - A human agent validates and owns the recommendation.
  - The demands-and-needs answers are recorded in structured form, with timestamp and agent ID.
  - Each AI suggestion is logged with its model version, inputs and output.
  - The reason a product was suggested can be explained in plain language.
  - Every suggestion is checked against the product's target market as defined in POG.
  - Guardrails stop the AI from suggesting upsell or ancillary products outside the customer's stated needs.
  - Inconsistencies in the data collected trigger alerts, as in the ACPR practice.
- If the AI optimises for conversion or commission rather than customer needs, that creates the conflict-of-interest and best-interest risk IDD Art. 17 targets. Incentive design should be reviewed.

### Gaps
- Full text of the 2025 Opinion (specific paragraphs on explainability towards customers and on human oversight of GenAI) could not be fetched.
- Whether EIOPA has issued 2026 follow-up work on GenAI or agentic AI in distribution: not verified.

## 3. United States: NAIC Model Bulletin (2023), state adoption, Colorado SB21-169

### Takeaway
The NAIC Model Bulletin was adopted on 4 Dec 2023. It requires a written AIS (AI systems) program covering governance, risk management, internal audit and third-party vendor oversight, and it applies across the insurance life cycle, including marketing and sales. About 24–25 states plus DC had adopted it by 2026. Colorado's SB21-169 and Regulation 10-1-1 add governance and unfair-discrimination testing for insurers using external consumer data and models. The scope now extends beyond life to auto and health.

### Cited Findings
- NAIC Model Bulletin: Use of Artificial Intelligence Systems by Insurers, adopted 4 Dec 2023. It is not binding until a state adopts it, and some states adopted it with modifications. The NAIC adoption map is dated 1 May 2026 — [NAIC adoption map](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/legal-adoption-map-ai-model-bulletin.pdf); [Quarles](https://quarles.com/newsroom/publications/nearly-half-of-states-have-now-adopted-naic-model-bulletin-on-insurers-use-of-ai)
- Adoption counts differ by source:
  - 25 states and DC: [Lumenova, Mar 2026](https://www.lumenova.ai/blog/naic-ai-model-bulletin-insurers-guide/)
  - at least 24 states and DC: [360factors](https://www.360factors.com/blog/ai-insurance-industry/)
  - 25 states, with 8 more in progress (vendor claim, July 2026): [Openlayer](https://www.openlayer.com/blog/post/naic-model-bulletin-ai-governance)
- The NAIC issued an RFI in 2025 on a possible Model Law on AI in insurance. It ran an AI evaluation tool pilot (CO, MD, CA, CT, FL, PA, VA) through Sept 2026, with adoption expected at the 2026 Fall National Meeting. In March 2026 it opposed federal preemption of state AI oversight — [360factors](https://www.360factors.com/blog/ai-insurance-industry/)
- Bulletin content: insurers must comply with unfair trade practices and unfair claims laws whatever tools they use. They must maintain a written AIS program proportionate to risk, with governance, risk management and internal controls, data and model documentation, and oversight of third-party AI vendors. Insurers should expect regulators to request documentation in examinations. The life cycle covered includes product development, marketing, sales and distribution, underwriting, pricing, claims and fraud. [primary text, from prior knowledge] — [NAIC Model Bulletin](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/2023-12-4%20Model%20Bulletin_Adopted_0.pdf)
- Colorado Regulation 10-1-1 (implementing SB21-169) covers insurers that use external consumer data and information sources (ECDIS) and the algorithms or models that use them. It requires board oversight, a model inventory, testing for unfair discrimination, monitoring and annual reporting. Rulemaking began in 2025 to extend it to private passenger auto and health benefit plans. Life insurers filed their second annual attestation on 1 Dec 2025. Effective dates conflict: one source says the amended regulation took effect on 15 Oct 2025, another gives 1 July 2026 for auto and health — [Faegre Drinker, May 2025](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/5/colorado-division-of-insurance-takes-next-steps-to-expand-ai-related-governance-and-risk-management-obligations-for-insurers); [Verifywise](https://verifywise.ai/blog/colorado-sb-21-169-compliance-playbook-for-insurers); [Milliman](https://www.milliman.com/fr-FR/insight/protecting-consumers-colorado-antidiscrimination-law-insurance)
- A vendor reports that Colorado SB 26-189 (signed 14 May 2026) replaces the Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) from 1 Jan 2027. This is unverified — [search summary of vendor sources](https://www.lumenova.ai/solutions/colorado-insurers/)

### Inferences
- The US model is a useful template for an internal "AI program" document: an inventory of AI uses in the subscription flow, a risk rating per use, vendor due diligence for the LLM and OCR providers, testing for proxy discrimination (for example, nationality or place of residence feeding product or price suggestions), and audit trails that can be handed to a regulator.

### Gaps
- The definitive state count as of Oct 2026 needs the NAIC map itself, which was not fetched in full.
- Outcome of the NAIC model-law process and the Fall 2026 meeting: unknown.

## 4. Morocco: Law 09-08 and CNDP, ACAPS and Law 17-99, BAM/ACAPS innovation, Digital X.0 / Maroc Digital 2030, data-protection reform, regional (CIMA)

### Takeaway
Morocco has no AI-specific law or ACAPS AI circular in force as of Oct 2026. The binding constraints are:
- **Law 09-08 and CNDP practice:**
  - prior declaration or authorisation of processing
  - authorisation for sensitive data such as health, and for transfers to non-adequate countries
  - a ban on decisions with legal effect based solely on automated profiling
  - a very restrictive CNDP position on biometrics and facial recognition, and on biometric databases hosted abroad
- **Law 17-99:** the Code des assurances (Book IV on distribution, plus the ACAPS circulars on information to policyholders)
- **ACAPS AML/KYC circular AS/02/19:** remote and electronic subscription is subject to the same identification conditions as face-to-face subscription.

Two reforms are pending: the "Digital X.0" framework law (AI, data governance, digital identity), at the SGG review stage, and the Book IV distribution reform, which strengthens the duty to advise, was filed with the SGG and targets publication in 2027. A modernised data-protection law aligned with the GDPR is discussed, but I found no tabled text.

### Cited Findings
**Law 09-08 / CNDP**
- Law 09-08 remains the applicable data-protection law in 2026. The CNDP cited its Art. 12 when reminding political parties of their obligations before the 23 Sept 2026 legislative elections. The CNDP ended its "awareness" phase in February 2025 and moved to enforcement, including dark-web leak detection with DEFENDIS. A reform to align 09-08 with the GDPR "has been under discussion for several years", with no timetable published — [RMG Solutions guide 2026](https://rmgsolutions.ma/fr/ressources/loi-09-08-protection-donnees-maroc-guide-2026); [H24info](https://h24info.ma/maroc/politique/legislatives-2026-donnees-personnelles-ia-la-cndp-fixe-les-regles/); [Lafrouji Avocats](https://lafroujiavocats.com/protection-donnees-maroc-cndp-maroc/); [TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/sponsors/legislation-faire-evoluer-la-loi-09-08-pour-mieux-nous-proteger_1904757)
- The CNDP's ten rules for parties (reported 31 Aug 2026) include contractual framing of cross-border transfers and flagging any AI-generated content. This is a signal of CNDP expectations on AI transparency, though outside insurance — [Bénin Web TV](https://beninwebtv.bj/maroc-la-cndp-fixe-les-regles-sur-les-donnees-personnelles-et-lia-avant-les-legislatives-de-2026/); [H24info](https://h24info.ma/maroc/politique/legislatives-2026-donnees-personnelles-ia-la-cndp-fixe-les-regles/)
- Key provisions of Law 09-08 [primary text, from prior knowledge]:
  - Art. 1 defines "sensitive data" to include health data.
  - Art. 4 requires consent unless another legal basis applies, such as performance of a contract.
  - Art. 5 sets out information duties at collection: controller identity, purposes, recipients, whether answers are mandatory, and rights.
  - Art. 7–9 give rights of access, rectification and opposition.
  - Art. 11 says no decision producing legal effects may be taken solely on the basis of automated processing intended to define the profile or evaluate aspects of the person's personality.
  - Art. 12 requires prior declaration, or prior authorisation for listed cases.
  - Art. 21 requires authorisation for processing sensitive or health data, use of the national ID number for other purposes, and interconnection of files.
  - Art. 43–44 restrict transfers to countries without adequate protection unless authorised or covered by an exception, such as consent or performance of a contract with the data subject.
  — [Loi 09-08 (CNDP)](https://www.cndp.ma/fr/loi-09-08/) (not fetched; cndp.ma unreachable from this session)
- Transfers to non-adequate countries require CNDP authorisation. The CNDP sets the list of adequate countries by deliberation; secondary sources cite the EU, UK, Switzerland and Canada. Remote access from abroad to a Moroccan-hosted database may itself count as a transfer. Vendor standard clauses (Google, Microsoft) may need a 09-08 addendum. Professional use of foreign LLM services is possible if the transfer is properly framed, with enterprise tiers that exclude training on customer data. These are secondary or commercial sources and need verification — [9anonai](https://9anonai.com/fr/blog/cndp-prior-authorization-data-transfer-2026); [CENIT 2026 guide](https://cenit.ma/articles/solutions-ia-saas-entreprises-maroc-guide-comparatif-2026/); [Jawhari, Mar 2026](https://avocat-jawhari.com/2026/03/25/intelligence-artificielle-maroc-donnees-personnelles-risques-cndp/)

**Biometrics and facial recognition**
- The CNDP imposed a moratorium on facial recognition (Deliberation D-194-2019, from 2 Sept 2019, 7 months) and extended it to 31 Dec 2020 (D-97-2020). Use-specific deliberations followed: D-108-EUS/2020 (remote bank account opening) and D-126-EUS/2020 (proof of life). The general framework is D-195-EUS/2020 of 30 Dec 2020. In D-195 the CNDP expresses strong reservations about each provider building its own biometric database of clients or prospects, "sometimes hosted outside national territory", and recommends a national trusted third party — [CNDP D-194-2019](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/deliberation-n-D-194-2019-30-08-2019.pdf); [CNDP D-108-EUS/2020](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/CNDP_Deliberation-n-D-108-2020_26-03-2020.pdf); [CNDP D-126-EUS/2020](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/CNDP_Deliberation-n-D-126-EUS-2020_29-07-2020.pdf); [CNDP D-195-EUS/2020](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/CNDP-Dlibration_D-195-EUS-2020-Usage_de_la_reconnaissance_faciale-_20210421.pdf); [Challenge.ma](https://www.challenge.ma/la-cndp-prolonge-le-moratoire-sur-la-reconnaissance-faciale-135203/)
- The CNDP notification procedure (published 24 July 2025) still refers to D-108-EUS-2020. In some cases it may require a written commitment not to use facial recognition — [CNDP Procédure de notification 2025](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/CNDP_Procedure-de-Notification-des-traitements_20250724.pdf) (search-summary only)
- The National Digital Identity Trusted Third Party (DGSN e-ID / CNIE verification) is the route the CNDP prefers. [inferred from D-195's recommendation; not separately sourced]

**Sanctions**
- Penalties under Law 09-08 are reported inconsistently, with fines of up to MAD 300,000 or MAD 600,000 cited and imprisonment possible in serious cases. Check the official text — [Lafrouji Avocats](https://lafroujiavocats.com/protection-donnees-maroc-cndp-maroc/); [Korte Law](https://korte-law.com/data-protection-privacy-law-morocco); [RecordingLaw](https://www.recordinglaw.com/world-laws/world-data-privacy-laws/morocco-data-privacy-laws/)

**ACAPS / Code des assurances (Law 17-99)**
- Law 17-99 has five books. Book IV governs "présentation des opérations d'assurance" (intermediaries). Practising as an intermediary requires ACAPS approval (agrément). ACAPS publishes a consolidated version (Feb 2025) — [ACAPS consolidated Code](https://www.acaps.ma/sites/default/files/2025-02/Loi_17-99_Code%20des%20assurances%20consolid%C3%A9_FR.pdf); [SGG draft Book IV note](https://www.sgg.gov.ma/portals/0/AvantProjet/119/Avp_loi_Refonte-livreIV_Fr.pdf)
- ACAPS Circular 01/AS/19 of 2 Jan 2019 implements parts of Law 17-99, including policyholder information duties such as the annual information to the subscriber (Art. 72). It was amended by a circular of 16 March 2021, published in the BO on 21 July 2022 — [ACAPS Circular 01/AS/19](https://www.acaps.ma/sites/default/files/2024-11/circulaire_de_lautorite_ndeg_01_as_19_du_02_janvier_2019_prise_pour_lapplication_de_certaines_dispositions_du_code_des_assurances_0.pdf); [ACAPS news, amendment of general circular](https://www.acaps.ma/en/news/amendment-acaps-general-circular)
- ACAPS AML/CFT Circular AS/02/19, Art. 20: remote subscription requests, including electronic ones, are subject to the same conditions as Arts. 13–17 (customer identification and verification) — [ACAPS Circular AS/02/19](https://www.acaps.ma/sites/default/files/circulaire_as-02-19_fr.pdf)
- Book IV reform: in 2023 the ACAPS president presented a reform of distribution — [Médias24, July 2023](https://medias24.com/2023/07/08/le-president-de-lacaps-detaille-la-reforme-reglementaire-de-la-distribution-des-produits-dassurance/). The ACAPS president later announced that the amended Book IV had been filed with the SGG, targeting publication in 2027. Its aims are to modernise distribution, strengthen the duty to advise and open the sector to innovation. The current Book IV text dates from 2002. I found no confirmation of adoption by the Conseil de gouvernement, and the exact date of the filing announcement is uncertain (around 2026) — [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualites-marocaines/assurances-reforme-livre-iv-acaps-depot-sgg-maroc); [EcoActu interview](https://ecoactu.ma/entretien-abderrahim-chaffai-acaps-vise-le-cap-2027-pour-le-nouveau-livre-iv/); [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-financiere-maroc/refonte-du-livre-iv-du-code-des-assurances-un-projet-stabilise-d-ici-la-fin-de-l-annee)

**Regional**
- CIMA (14 mainly francophone West and Central African states): I found no AI or digital-distribution regulation. In Nov 2025 the CIMA chief urged supervisors to "understand and live with" AI and insurtech before regulating — [AFIS, Nov 2025](https://www.afis.africa/en/2025/11/24/)
- Québec AMF draft guideline on AI use by financial institutions: July 2025, consultation closed Nov 2025 (comparator) — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/fr/insights/2025/07/quebecs-autorite-des-marches-financiers-moves-on-ai-oversight-for-financial-institutions)
- The IAIS Application Paper on the supervision of AI (2 July 2025) is supporting material, not new standards; no changes to the ICPs are needed. It covers risk-based supervision and proportionality, governance and accountability, robustness, safety and security, transparency and explainability, and fairness, ethics and redress. A members-only report on agentic AI is due early 2027. ACAPS is an IAIS member, so the paper is the most directly relevant international benchmark for a Moroccan supervisor — [IAIS press release](https://www.iais.org/2025/07/the-iais-publishes-application-paper-on-the-supervision-of-artificial-intelligence/); [IAIS paper PDF](https://www.dirittobancario.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Documento-applicativo-IAIS-02-luglio-2025.pdf); [IAIS webinar slides](https://www.iais.org/uploads/2025/07/Final-AI-AP-public-background-webinar-July-17.pdf)

### Inferences
Implications for Africa First Assist's AI-assisted subscription flow. All of these need validation by a Moroccan lawyer and the DPO.

1. **CNDP formalities before go-live.**
   - Update or file the CNDP declaration for the subscription processing.
   - Seek authorisation if the flow processes health data, for example medical questions for travel medical cover or pre-existing conditions. Health data is sensitive, so this is likely.
   - Seek authorisation if CIN numbers are reused beyond identification or files are interconnected.
   - Seek transfer authorisation (or confirm an exception applies) if an LLM or OCR provider processes data outside Morocco or the CNDP-adequate list. That includes US-hosted APIs and remote access from abroad.
2. **ID-card OCR.** Plain OCR text extraction from the CIN or passport is ordinary personal-data processing; data minimisation applies, so capture only the fields needed and avoid storing full images unless required by AML (Circular AS/02/19 requires identification and retention of copies). Face matching or liveness checks would trigger the facial-recognition and biometric regime: CNDP deliberations D-195/D-108, a likely authorisation requirement, and CNDP hostility to provider-held or foreign-hosted biometric databases. Avoid facial biometrics in v1, or route verification through the national trusted third party.
3. **What must stay human.** Under Law 09-08 Art. 11, no decision producing legal effects, such as refusing cover or setting individual terms, may rest solely on automated profiling. Under the Code des assurances, the approved intermediary or insurer remains responsible for the advice. The agent therefore validates the product choice, any refusal or exclusion, and the final contract. The AI suggests and the human decides.
4. **What must be explained or disclosed to the customer.**
   - The Law 09-08 Art. 5 information notice: purposes including AI-assisted processing, recipients including the AI vendor, transfers abroad, and rights.
   - The Code des assurances pre-contractual information on guarantees, exclusions and premium.
   - Best practice, aligned with EU Art. 50 and the CNDP's 2026 AI-content flagging stance: disclose that AI assists the agent, and label any AI-generated document.
5. **What must be recorded.**
   - The KYC evidence required by ACAPS AS/02/19, the same for remote and face-to-face subscription.
   - The needs captured and why the product was proposed. This anticipates the Book IV reform's stronger duty to advise, consistent with IDD Art. 20 practice.
   - Logs of AI suggestions and agent overrides, consent records, and transfer bases.
6. **Consent.** The contract legal basis covers core subscription processing. Separate opt-in consent is needed for marketing or profiling, and explicit consent is advisable for health data and for any transfer abroad relying on the consent exception.
7. **Forward-looking risk.** The Digital X.0 law, the Book IV reform (target 2027) and a possible 09-08 overhaul could add requirements on algorithm governance, the duty to advise and digital distribution. ACAPS's planned AI-based monitoring of online sales means AI-generated marketing or sales scripts are likely to be scrutinised.

## 5. Documented enforcement cases or supervisory statements on AI in insurance sales (any jurisdiction)

### Takeaway
I found no regulator fine or sanction specifically for AI-driven insurance mis-selling. The closest precedents are liability for chatbot statements (Air Canada, 2024) and supervisory statements: the EIOPA Opinion, the IAIS paper, the NAIC Bulletin, FINRA's 2026 report on hallucinations, and ACAPS's plan for AI-based monitoring of online sales.

### Cited Findings
- Moffatt v. Air Canada (BC Civil Resolution Tribunal, 2024): the company was held liable for its chatbot's incorrect statement about bereavement fares (about C$812), and the tribunal rejected the argument that the bot was a separate entity. Courts increasingly hold companies to what their chatbots say — [PYMNTS 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/chatbot-tracker/2026/courts-tell-companies-they-own-what-their-chatbot-says/)
- FINRA's 2026 oversight report (securities, not insurance) flags hallucinations and AI agents acting beyond the user's intended scope as compliance concerns — [search summary; McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/ai-regulation-in-insurance-a-crossroads/)
- Insurers are distributing inside ChatGPT (Liberty Mutual reportedly writes new business there), while producer-licensing and commission rules have "no regulatory answer" for sales without an agent — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/cyber/insurers-race-into-chatgpt-while-agent-rules-stay-silent-587828.aspx)
- Colorado's Chatbot Safety Act (effective 1 Jan 2027) restricts presenting chatbot outputs as equivalent to licensed professional services (single secondary source) — [search summary](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/ai-regulation-in-insurance-a-crossroads/)

### Inferences
- In practice, the main enforcement exposure for an AI-assisted flow is conventional: mis-selling, inaccurate pre-contractual information, data-protection breaches (CNDP formalities, transfers) and KYC failures. The use of AI does not shift liability away from the insurer or intermediary. The Air Canada reasoning would apply to an AI-generated quote or coverage explanation.

### Gaps
- The search found no specific insurance-sector AI enforcement cases from the UK FCA, US state DOIs or EU national competent authorities. Examples worth checking but not verified here: US litigation over AI claims denials (UnitedHealth/naviHealth, Cigna PxDx, which concern claims rather than sales), Italian Garante actions on AI chatbots, and state DOI market-conduct exams. A targeted follow-up is needed before claiming there are none.
