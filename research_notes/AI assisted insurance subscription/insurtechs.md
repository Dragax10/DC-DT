# AI-native insurers, brokers, MGAs and insurtech vendors: the human–AI split in distribution, quoting, submission intake and underwriting (2022–2026)

Note on evidence quality: the research ran on a budget of about 17 tool calls, and medium.com did not resolve from the research environment. Most metrics below are company-reported, from press releases, vendor pages or trade-press rewrites. Items marked **[Verified/filed]** come from SEC filings or court records; items marked **[Company claim]** have not been independently checked.

## Q1. Customer-facing AI agents (Lemonade, Hippo, Root, Next, Getsafe, Wakam, Alan, Luko): how much is automated, where humans step in, results and complaints

### Takeaway
Lemonade is the only company here that publishes audited automation figures. AI Jim takes about 96% of first notices of loss (FNOL) with no human, and about 55% of claims are automated end to end. Humans take any claim AI Jim is not authorized to settle or flags. Lemonade's wording has also become more cautious: since FY2024 the 10-K no longer says AI Jim "pays or declines" claims. Its most-cited failure was the 2021 "non-verbal cues" episode, which ended in a $4M BIPA settlement. The other names have little public, verifiable data on their automation.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified/filed] Lemonade FY2025 10-K:** "as of December 31, 2025, 96% of the time, it is AI Jim that will take the first notice of loss... without human intervention (and with zero... LAE)"; "roughly 55% of our claims were automated." — [Lemonade 10-K FY2025 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691421/000169142126000016/lmnd-20251231.htm)
- **[Verified/filed] Same figures a year earlier:** the FY2024 10-K reports the same 96% and roughly 55% as of Dec 31, 2024. The FY2023 10-K said 98%, and that version described AI Jim as also paying or declining claims without human intervention. The 2024–2025 wording drops the pay/decline language. — [10-K FY2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691421/000169142125000031/lmnd-20241231.htm); [10-K FY2023](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691421/000169142124000026/lmnd-20231231.htm)
- **[Verified/filed] Escalation rule:** AI Jim triages and assigns claims he is not authorized to settle, or where he identifies concerns, to human claims experts. — [Lemonade 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691421/000169142126000016/lmnd-20251231.htm)
- **Lemonade facial-analysis controversy (May 2021):** Lemonade tweeted that it analyzes "non-verbal cues" in claim videos, then retracted and called it "a bad choice of words." It said facial recognition is used to flag claims filed by the same person under different identities. — [Carlton Fields](https://carltonfields.com/insights/expect-focus/2022/ai-insurance-company-faces-class-action-for-use-of); [Insurance Business](https://insurancebusinessmag.com/ca/news/technology/lemonade-faces-class-action-over-alleged-mishandling-of-biometric-data-308315.aspx)
- **[Verified/court] BIPA settlement (2022):** Lemonade agreed to pay $4M to settle claims under the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act that it collected biometric data from video claim submissions without consent. The class covers video FNOL submitters from June 25, 2019 to May 27, 2021. Lemonade agreed to delete the biometric identifiers, did not admit wrongdoing, and the court granted final approval in Aug 2022. Sources disagree on the split: one says 75% Illinois / 25% national, another says $3M / $1M. — [Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/closed-settlements/lemonade-insurance-biometric-privacy-4m-class-action-settlement/); [Exterro](https://www.exterro.com/resources/data-privacy-alerts/insurance-company-settles-bipa-claim-for-4-million)
- **"Maya":** Maya is Lemonade's onboarding and sales chatbot. The searches found no lawsuits or complaints that name it; the BIPA litigation concerns AI Jim and video claims. — [Exterro](https://www.exterro.com/resources/data-privacy-alerts/insurance-company-settles-bipa-claim-for-4-million)
- **Alan (France, health):** Alan's engineering blog describes a "Claim Agent," a ReAct-style LLM agent that looks up care-event details, document status and coverage allowances to answer members' claim questions. Its first release fully automated about 30% of the claims-related support tickets it received. The post dates from around 2025. I could not fetch the full text to check its escalation design. — [Alan Medium blog (via search snippet)](https://medium.com/alan/inside-alans-claim-agent-how-an-ai-investigates-claims-just-like-a-human-only-faster-11d62bd7efc9)
- **Root:** Root runs an API-first embedded business in which partners such as Carvana integrate quoting, checkout and servicing into their own purchase flows. It operates in 36 states, about 80% of the US population, per its Q4 2025 call. The CEO has described a goal of a "fully automated, AI-based, closed-loop" platform. Root reported its best quarterly net income ever, nearly $36M, in Q1 2026. — [Q4 2025 call transcript](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/root-inc-nasdaqroot-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript-1705154/); [Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/05/07/868894.htm)
- **Hippo:** Hippo built its brand on near-instant home quotes from third-party data and AI; its early round was $70M. It now also rents out its licenses and infrastructure to other insurers ("insurance as a service"). It reported a profit in Q3 and year to date after losses in 2024. — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/home-insurance-startup-gets-70-million-for-almostinstant-ai-quote-system-116328.aspx); [eMarketer](https://emarketer.com/content/lemonade-root-earnings-insurtechs-scale)
- **Industry comparison:** Travelers reportedly has more than half of its claims eligible for straight-through processing, and customers choose the automated path about two-thirds of the time. This comes from a secondary blog and is unverified. — [buildmvpfast blog](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/ai-insurance-agents-claims-underwriting-automation-2026)

### Inferences
- Lemonade's pattern is AI at intake plus authority-limited auto-settlement, with humans handling anything outside the authority limit or flagged. Its public language moved from "AI pays or declines" (FY2023) to "AI takes FNOL; ~55% automated" (FY2024–25). That fits a retreat from implying fully automated denials, which is the regulatory and litigation hot zone; the shift is my reading, not something Lemonade says.
- The 55% figure is identical in FY2024 and FY2025. Either automation has plateaued or the number is being carried forward.
- Alan's ~30% of tickets is a reasonable benchmark for what LLM service agents fully resolve; the rest go to humans.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable 2022–2026 sources on Getsafe, Wakam or Luko automation rates. Luko went into receivership and was acquired by Allianz Direct in 2024; that comes from background knowledge and was not verified here.
- I found nothing on Next Insurance's AI quoting. Its acquisition by Ergo/Munich Re in 2025 for about $2.6B is background knowledge, not verified here.
- No NPS, complaint-ratio or regulator-complaint data turned up for any of these companies.
- I could not access Lemonade's shareholder-letter wording, or its loss-ratio trend tied to AI.

## Q2. AI brokers, MGAs and agentic brokerage (Harper, Corgi, Pibit.ai, Ominimo, Marshmallow, Bolttech, Indemn, Coalition, YC "AI agent for agencies")

### Takeaway
2025–2026 saw full-stack "AI-native" distributors raise very large rounds. These are licensed agencies and carriers that run on AI internally, not software sold to brokers. Corgi went from about $630M to a reported $4B valuation in under a year, and Harper raised $47M. Their human–AI split is described only in marketing terms. AI reads applications, routes submissions, chases underwriters and talks to clients, while licensed humans handle accounts and carrier relationships. None of them publish bind-rate or loss-ratio data.

### Cited Findings
- **Harper (YC W25; AI-native commercial brokerage):** raised about $47M (seed plus Series A) led by Emergence Capital, with YC, Peak XV, Antler and others; $54M in total. It positions itself as a licensed agency, not a vendor. Its AI handles application reading, submission routing, underwriter follow-up and client communications. It claims coverage in 24–48 hours "instead of weeks," more than 5,000 customers and more than 165 carriers, across workers' comp, general liability and professional liability. Funding is going to engineering, account management and operations, so humans stay in the loop. **[Company claim]** — [fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/02/26/harper-raises-47m-to-modernise-business-insurance-with-ai/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/harper-insure-47-million-series-a-raised-to-build-the-insurance-company-every-business-deserves); [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/y-combinator-grad-harper-raises-47m-for-ai-native-insurance-brokerage)
- **Harper and voice AI:** a podcast describes Harper using voice AI from quote through claims, after pivoting from selling AI tools to operating as a broker. — [Untapped Ventures](https://untappedventures.substack.com/p/how-ai-agents-cut-insurance-costs-26d)
- **Corgi (YC; AI-native full-stack carrier for startups):** raised $108M in Jan 2026 (seed plus Series A, with YC, Kindred and Contrary) after getting full regulatory approval as a carrier in July 2025. It claims ARR above $40M. — [ClickClaims](https://www.clickclaims.com/2026/01/12/corgi-raises-108m-launches-ai-driven-full-stack-carrier-for-startups/); [Insurance Business](https://insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/corgi-lands-carrier-approval-to-scale-aipowered-startup-insurance-561619.aspx)
  - **Later rounds:** a $160M Series B led by TCV at $1.3B (May 2026, expanding into trucking), a $106M B1 at $2.6B about three weeks later, and a reported B2 at $4B (sources cited by Forbes; Corgi declined comment). It has also moved into data-room software and runs two coffee shops. — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/corgi-160m-series-b-tcv-13bn-valuation-trucking); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/140973-corgi-raises-again-at-4b-valuation-its-third-round-since-may/)
  - **Source conflict:** TNW gave the Series A valuation as $630M, while TechCrunch called it undisclosed.
- **Ominimo (Hungary/Serbia; AI-priced motor insurance):** reached unicorn status about two years after launch at a €1.4B ($1.6B) valuation, with a Series B involving EBRD and a reported €20.1M raise. Zurich earlier invested €10M for a 5% stake. It says it is profitable. PitchBook gives a $22.5M deal on 27 Jul 2026. — [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/07/profitable-serbian-hungarian-insurtech-ominimo-hits-unicorn-status-two-years-after-launch-raises-e20-1-million-at-e1-4-billion-valuation/); [IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/ominimo-reaches-1-6bn-valuation-after-series-b-funding-round/); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/773227-81)
- **Marshmallow (UK motor; data-driven pricing for migrants):** reported a $90M raise at a $2B valuation led by Portage. Tracxn lists a Series C on 14 Apr 2025 at $45M. The amounts and dates conflict across aggregators and should be checked against a primary source. — [TechResearchOnline](https://techresearchonline.com/news/marshmallow-expansion-insurance-funding/); [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/marshmallow/__e9G6xiDh1gIMIWdr2hzTTvMhoZUlOOe4fk9YaTdv6sY)
- **bolttech "bolt AI for Agencies" (launched 15 Oct 2025):** a voice and workflow agent for P&C agencies. It answers inbound calls, completes data intake and generates "real-time bindable quotes," and is marketed as cutting quoting time and improving bind rates. **[Company claim; no figures found]** — [fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2025/10/21/bolt-ai-for-agencies-transforms-insurance-quoting-process/); [bolttech](https://bolttech.io/sales/ai-for-agencies/)
- **Indemn (NY, founded 2021):** a conversational AI platform covering quote to purchase. It raised a $1.9M pre-seed led by Markd (Jan 2024). In June 2024 it launched "Agent Copilot," which suggests response recommendations to human agents during live chats. This is a human-in-the-loop copilot pattern, not an autonomous agent. — [Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/east/2024/02/01/758902.htm); [Coverager](https://coverager.com/indemn-raises-1-9-million/)
- **Commodity voice agents:** white-label voice AI for agencies is sold from $99/month (Trillet), which suggests the plain "AI answers the phone and does intake" layer is already cheap. — [Trillet](https://trillet.ai/blogs/voice-agent-for-insurance-agencies)

### Inferences
- **Two models:** (a) AI-native risk-bearers or brokers that keep the margin (Harper, Corgi, Ominimo, Marshmallow), and (b) copilots and voice agents sold to existing agencies (bolt, Indemn, Trillet). Investors valued (a) much more highly in 2025–26.
- **Where humans sit:** the AI does the clerical middle — reading applications, routing to carriers, follow-ups. Licensed humans keep binding authority with carriers and the client relationship, which is the clearest pattern from Harper. None of these firms publicly says which approvals humans must give.

### Gaps
- Pibit.ai: no results. It is known from background as an AI underwriting and submission-intake vendor for commercial P&C; not verified here.
- Coalition (cyber MGA, active risk monitoring): not researched in this pass.
- I found no YC directory list of "AI agent for insurance agencies" startups; the search was not productive.
- No company here published bind rates, loss ratios, error rates or E&O incidents tied to AI.

## Q3. Underwriting and submission AI for humans (Sixfold, Federato, Kalepa, hyperexponential, Cytora, Gradient AI, Shift, Zelros, Tractable, Planck, Send): what UX do underwriters get?

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is decision support with citations: the AI extracts, triages and recommends, and the human underwriter decides. Sixfold links each finding to the source document and the relevant manual provision, and lets the insurer set how much human review is required. Cytora routes work to humans based on configurable confidence thresholds. Federato and Kalepa rank submissions by appetite and likelihood to bind, and Shift took "agentic" claims tooling to market in 2025. The UX claims come from vendors, and I found no independent accuracy testing of the citations.

### Cited Findings
- **Sixfold:** the AI Underwriter reviews evidence, applies the insurer's manual, and shows its reasoning with citations to source documents and manual provisions. In P&C it uses "explainable signals" that are traceable to the source document. Underwriters "review the recommendation, inspect citations and apply their own judgment before taking action," and insurers decide how much human review is required. It launched a Life & Health version in Sep 2026 and claims a 55% cut in case evaluation time. **[Company claim]** — [fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/02/sixfold-launches-ai-underwriter-for-life-and-health/); [Insurance Edge](https://insurance-edge.net/2026/09/03/sixfold-launches-ai-underwriter-for-life-health/); [Beinsure](https://beinsure.com/news/sixfold-launches-ai-underwriter-life-health-insurance/)
- **Cytora:** turns incoming submissions (email, PDFs) into "decision-ready data." Its LLM-powered "risk flows" bring in a risk professional based on conditions that include confidence thresholds, and human review rules can be set per confidence threshold to handle exceptions. It works with Arch, but I found no quantified results. **[Company claim]** — [Cytora configurable risk flows](https://cytora.com/configurable-risk-flows); [Cytora](https://www.cytora.com); [Digital Risk Processing](https://cytora.com/digital-risk-processing)
- **Federato (RiskOps):** helps underwriters triage submissions and rank in-appetite risks by appetite, guidelines and "winnability." The CEO, in a podcast, claimed response times cut by up to 89%. It raised a $100M Series D led by Goldman Sachs Alternatives (Nov 2025), for more than $180M in total, and says revenue more than tripled in a year. **[Company claim]** — [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/federato-raises-100m-series-d-to-accelerate-global-expansion/); [Coverager](https://coverager.com/federato-raises-100-million/); [InsTech podcast](https://instechlondon.podbean.com/e/will-ross-ceo-co-founder-federato-driving-underwriting-with-riskops-343)
- **Kalepa (Copilot):** in a mid-market carrier case study, out-of-appetite risks were triaged out automatically, underwriters focused on the submissions most likely to bind, and the carrier then embedded Copilot in its workflow. No numbers were given. Kalepa's last disclosed raise was $14M (2021), per CB Insights. — [Kalepa case study](https://www.kalepa.com/case-study/mid-market-carrier); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/federato-vs-kalepa)
- **hyperexponential (hx):** pricing and decision platform for specialty and commercial lines. It launched "hx AI" to help actuaries and underwriters build models. — [Rocketlist profile](https://rocketlist.ai/companies/hyperexponential)
- **Shift Technology:** added generative AI to its products in 2024 and "agentic" capabilities in 2025. "Shift Claims" (Sep 2025) assesses and prioritizes cases, guides claim handlers and automates tasks, which is a guide-the-human design. It extends earlier fraud tooling into underwriting. — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/shift-technology-launches-shift-claims-to-power-claims-transformation-with-agentic-ai-302557099.html); [Shift](https://www.shift-technology.com/resources/reports-and-insights/agentic-ai-is-the-future-and-the-future-is-now)

### Inferences
Across vendors, the UX converges on four elements:
1. Structured extraction from messy submissions.
2. Appetite triage and a ranked queue.
3. A recommendation with source citations or explainable signals.
4. Insurer-configurable confidence thresholds that decide when a human must review.

Final bind authority stays with a human underwriter in every vendor I found. "Explain via citation to the document and the guideline" is the de-facto standard for explaining recommendations.

### Gaps
- No usable results on Gradient AI, Zelros, Tractable, Planck or Send in this pass. From background: Planck is now part of Applied Systems, Send is a UK underwriting workbench, and Tractable does computer-vision damage appraisal. None of this was verified here.
- I found no independent benchmark of citation accuracy, hallucination rates, or underwriter override rates for any vendor.

## Q4. Published metrics (quote time, bind rate, capacity, loss ratio, NPS) and documented failures or lawsuits

### Takeaway
The hard, filed numbers are Lemonade's 96% AI FNOL and about 55% automated claims. Everything else (Harper's 24–48 hours, Federato's up to 89% faster responses, Sixfold's 55% faster evaluations, Alan's ~30% automated tickets) is self-reported. The documented failures sit in claims, not underwriting: Lemonade's BIPA settlement, Cigna PxDx and UnitedHealth nH Predict. These cases target automated denials with too little human review, which is why vendors keep humans as the final decision-makers.

### Cited Findings
- **Metrics summary:**
  - Lemonade: 96% FNOL without humans; about 55% of claims automated **[filed]**. — [10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1691421/000169142126000016/lmnd-20251231.htm)
  - Harper: coverage in 24–48 hours versus weeks **[claim]**. — [fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2026/02/26/harper-raises-47m-to-modernise-business-insurance-with-ai/)
  - Federato: response times up to 89% faster **[claim]**. — [InsTech](https://instechlondon.podbean.com/e/will-ross-ceo-co-founder-federato-driving-underwriting-with-riskops-343)
  - Sixfold: 55% less case evaluation time **[claim]**. — [Beinsure](https://beinsure.com/news/sixfold-launches-ai-underwriter-life-health-insurance/)
  - Alan: ~30% of claim tickets automated **[claim]**. — [Alan blog](https://medium.com/alan/inside-alans-claim-agent-how-an-ai-investigates-claims-just-like-a-human-only-faster-11d62bd7efc9)
  - Corgi: ARR above $40M **[claim]**. — [ClickClaims](https://www.clickclaims.com/2026/01/12/corgi-raises-108m-launches-ai-driven-full-stack-carrier-for-startups/)
- **[Court] Cigna PxDx (Kisting-Leung v. Cigna, E.D. Cal.):** on 31 Mar 2025 the judge let a proposed class action proceed in part. The court found Cigna's reading of its plan terms, which it relied on to use the algorithm, to be an abuse of discretion. Cigna says PxDx is not AI and is similar to tools CMS uses. — [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/judge-advances-class-claims-over-cigna-use-of-automated-algorithm-to-deny-benefits/); [NFP](https://www.nfp.com/insights/court-allows-lawsuit-over-ai-use-in-benefit-denials-to-proceed/)
- **[Court] UnitedHealth nH Predict:** a March 2026 discovery order required production of internal documents on nH Predict; there has been no merits ruling. UnitedHealth says the tool guides care coordination and does not make coverage decisions. Advocacy coverage claims about 90% of appealed denials were reversed; that is a plaintiff allegation, not verified. — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/daraabasiita/2026/06/09/the-algorithm-that-counted-on-no-one-appealing/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/ai-algorithm-based-health-insurer-denials-pose-new-legal-threat)
- **Lemonade BIPA:** $4M settlement (2022), described in Q1. — [Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/closed-settlements/lemonade-insurance-biometric-privacy-4m-class-action-settlement/)
- **Unreliable secondary figures:** advocacy and law-firm blogs circulate figures such as "1.2 seconds per claim" (PxDx, from ProPublica's 2023 reporting) and "300,000 denials." Check them against the filings before use. — [alignmt.ai](https://www.alignmt.ai/post/from-556m-to-1-2-seconds-the-healthcare-ai-cases-that-changed-everything-in-2026)

### Inferences
- The litigation risk is concentrated in automated adverse decisions, meaning denials, with only nominal human review. That explains the convergent design: AI may auto-approve or auto-pay within limits, while declines and edge cases go to humans. Lemonade's 10-K wording change is consistent with this.
- Every underwriting and quote-to-bind metric found is about speed or effort, not outcomes. None covers loss ratio, bind-rate lift or NPS.

### Gaps
- I found no published bind rates, loss-ratio impact or NPS tied to AI underwriting from any vendor or MGA.
- State Farm algorithmic-claims litigation (Huskey v. State Farm, a disparate-impact case) was not verified in this pass.
- No E&O claims or regulatory actions against AI brokers or MGAs (Harper, Corgi) turned up.
- Regulatory guardrails, such as the NAIC AI model bulletin and Colorado SB21-169, were outside this pass's searches.
