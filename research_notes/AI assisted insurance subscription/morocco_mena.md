## 1. Moroccan market structure, distribution channels and key players (insurers and assistance)

### Takeaway
In 2024 Morocco's insurance market was about MAD 63 bn, with penetration around 3.7%. Distribution is split roughly three ways: bancassurance (31.9%), brokers (30.4%), and the agents-general plus direct-office networks (the rest, about 38% by subtraction). There are about 2,100–2,500 licensed intermediaries. The assistance segment is small and concentrated among roughly five companies. Africa First Assist (formerly Saham Assistance, a Sanlam subsidiary) was third in 2024 with about MAD 402 m.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 market turnover across all lines was MAD 63.1 bn (USD 6.1 bn), up 5.4% year on year, per ACAPS. Penetration slipped to 3.7% from 3.8% (joint Bank Al-Maghrib/ACAPS/AMMC report). (snippet) — [Atlas Magazine, bancassurance 2024](https://atlas-mag.net/fr/articles/marche-marocain-de-la-bancassurance-en-2024). Another outlet gives "close to MAD 59 bn (+5.1%)" for 2024, a discrepancy probably due to different perimeters (e.g., with or without reinsurance or Takaful) — [Challenge.ma](https://www.challenge.ma/le-paradoxe-du-courtage-marocain-indispensable-mais-sous-estime-324638/)
- Bancassurance turnover in 2024 was MAD 20.5 bn (+6.6% vs MAD 19.2 bn in 2023), and 96.1% of it is life/savings. At 31 Dec 2024, bancassurance held 31.9% of the market and brokerage 30.4%. (snippet) — [Atlas Magazine](https://atlas-mag.net/fr/articles/marche-marocain-de-la-bancassurance-en-2024)
- Brokers placed MAD 19.5 bn of premiums in 2024 and rank second, ahead of agents-general and direct offices ("bureaux de gestion directe"). (snippet) — [Challenge.ma](https://www.challenge.ma/le-paradoxe-du-courtage-marocain-indispensable-mais-sous-estime-324638/)

A FNACAM meeting reported by Hespress in Oct 2026 gives 2,154 intermediaries (1,624 agents and 530 brokers). The sources conflict, and the perimeter or date likely differs. (snippet) — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/491667-assurance-elargir-le-marche-mais-avec-quels-intermediaires.html)
- Older baseline (pre-2022): at end-2017 there were 1,864 agents and brokers plus 463 direct offices. ### Inferences
- About 38% of premiums go through agents-general and direct offices. Because bancassurance is mostly life business, agents, brokers and direct offices dominate non-life lines, which include assistance and travel. An agent-facing tool therefore targets the channel that carries most P&C and assistance sales.
- Africa First Assist ranks about third in Moroccan assistance by turnover. It has lost relative share since 2016, when as Saham Assistance it led with 38.8%. Front-office productivity tools could be part of a response to that.
- "ISAAF" appears in the press as "Isaaf Mondial Assistance" (linked to Mondial Assistance, i.e. Allianz Partners). It distributes the FNAVM Hajj product (see section 2). The brief's pairing "Africa First Assist / ISAAF" should be double-checked, because the sources found treat them as separate entities.

## 2. Travel insurance in Morocco: Schengen, Hajj/Umrah, MRE, channels and prices

### Takeaway
Schengen visa demand is the biggest structural driver of travel insurance, with about 600k Moroccan applications in 2024 and a refusal rate of about 20%. Medical travel insurance is mandatory for the visa, and the market standard is cover of at least €30k. Hajj and Umrah are a second pillar, sold through travel agencies (FNAVM) with partners such as Isaaf Mondial Assistance. Prices are low: from tens to a few hundred MAD for a short trip.

### Cited Findings
- About 606k Schengen visas were granted or applied for in 2024 (+17% vs 2023). Moroccans spent nearly MAD 1 bn on Schengen visas in 2024, and refused applicants lost more than MAD 200 m. Hespress reports about 20% refusals. (snippet) — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/438278-les-marocains-ont-debourse-pres-d1-milliard-dh-pour-des-visas-schengen-en-2024.html)
- HelloSafe gives about 606,800 applications and a 20.1% refusal rate — [HelloSafe](https://hellosafe.com/fr/schengen-visa/from-morocco). A Turespaña-cited figure in Bladi gives 437,029 visas granted, which conflicts with the figures above; it is probably granted versus applied — [Bladi](https://www.bladi.net/visas-schengen-marocains-gros-demandeurs-monde,121943.html)
- The Schengen visa fee rose from €80 to €90 on 11 June 2024 — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/368863-frais-de-visa-schengen-hausse-et-implications-pour-les-voyageurs.html)
- Travel medical insurance of at least €30k covering medical care, hospitalisation and repatriation is required for the visa. This comes from a commercial source — [HelloSafe Schengen insurance Morocco](https://hellosafe.com/fr/schengen-visa/travel-insurance/from-morocco). The Spanish consulate in Rabat requires multiple-entry visa holders to carry valid travel medical insurance on each entry — [Exteriores.gob.es](https://exteriores.gob.es/DocumentosAuxiliaresSC/Marruecos/RABAT%20(C)/Requisitos%20visados/FRA/1.VISA%20SCHENGEN%20POUR%20TOURISME%20%20VISITE%20À%20LA%20FAMILLE%20%20AFFAIRES.pdf)
- A 2023 complaint by a consumer federation said Moroccan insurers do not refund travel insurance when a visa is refused. This is a customer-friction point (snippet via HelloSafe/Hespress search summary) — [HelloSafe](https://hellosafe.com/fr/schengen-visa/travel-insurance/from-morocco)
- Hajj: the Fédération nationale des agences de voyages du Maroc (FNAVM) launched an optional cancellation/interruption assistance product for Hajj, distributed with Isaaf Mondial Assistance. The premium is 2% incl. tax of the package price, plus 14% tax on the pre-tax product cost. It was announced on Wednesday 2 July, likely 2025. (snippet) — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/441379-hajj-et-omra-mesures-renforcees-pour-proteger-les-pelerins-marocains.html)
- In Sept 2025 the Ministry of Tourism said Hajj/Umrah agencies are now selected on strict competence and financial-guarantee criteria — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/441379-hajj-et-omra-mesures-renforcees-pour-proteger-les-pelerins-marocains.html)
- In Feb 2026 a consumer observatory urged stronger control of Umrah agencies after a fraud arrest in Marrakech — [Hespress FR](https://fr.hespress.com/462180-omra-lobservatoire-du-consommateur-appelle-a-un-controle-renforce-des-agences.html)
- Indicative prices from a commercial comparison site, unverified: about MAD 150–300 for a 15-day Umrah and MAD 400–700 for a 30-day Hajj. The same site claims Saudi Arabia has required health cover of at least SAR 100k for pilgrims since 2023, which was not verified with an official source — [Wafir Hajj/Omra](https://wafir.ma/fr/assurance-hajj-omra); [Wafir Omra](https://wafir.ma/fr/assurance-voyage-destination/arabie-saoudite-omra)
- MRE (Moroccans living abroad) travel and repatriation products are marketed as a distinct segment — [Wafir MRE](https://wafir.ma/fr/mre/assurance-voyage)

### Inferences
- Schengen demand is document-heavy and deadline-driven. Customers need a compliant certificate (dates, €30k, Schengen zone) for a visa appointment, often through the intermediary VFS/BLS circuits. A tool that pre-fills from passport/CIN data and produces an attestation that meets consulate requirements addresses a real, high-volume pain point.
- Refusal rates are about 20% and refunds after refusal are contested. Clear disclosure of refund and cancellation terms at subscription is a compliance and trust need.
- Hajj/Umrah sales go through travel agencies, which suggests B2B2C channels that an agent tool may need to support.

### Gaps
- No ACAPS or FMSAR figure was found for travel insurance premium volume, or for the split between travel agencies, insurance agencies and online sales.
- No official Africa First Assist travel price list was found.
- No source specifically covered the MRE summer season (Marhaba operation) and assistance volumes.

## 3. AI and digital initiatives by Moroccan and MENA insurers and insurtechs

### Takeaway
Morocco is early-stage. The regulatory scaffolding exists: the ACAPS online-sales instruction (in force July 2022) and an ACAPS–AfDB insurtech programme running to 2028. A few visible launches stand out: AtlantaSanad's "Assia" AI assistant (Sept 2025), Wafa Assurance's Digital Factory, La Marocaine Vie's fully online subscription (awarded Feb 2025), and the RMA–UM6P insurtech open innovation programme (Apr 2026). Online sales still lag. The GCC is ahead in MENA, with aggregators such as Tameeni and Yallacompare and generative-AI framing at industry summits.

### Cited Findings
- AtlantaSanad launched "Assia," presented as the first AI assistant dedicated to insurance in Morocco. It answers client questions and guides claims and subscription, and is available on the website rather than WhatsApp (Sept 2025) — [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/atlantasanad-devoile-assia-premiere-assistante-ia-dediee-a-lassurance/302166); [FNH](https://fnh.ma/articles/atlantasanad-ia-assia-assistante)
- Wafa Assurance launched a Digital Factory to digitise about 40 customer journeys. The team includes AI specialists and data scientists, and Devoteam supported the project (date about 2020–21, unclear) — [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/laquotidienne/wafa-assurance-lance-sa-digital-factory); [EcoActu](https://www.ecoactu.ma/wafa-assurance-digital-factory/)

## 4. Languages, channels, e-signature (Law 43-20) and digital ID (CNIE)

### Takeaway
WhatsApp is the dominant channel: 75% of the population use it and 95% of those use it daily. Law 43-20 (2020/21) creates simple, advanced and qualified e-signatures, and only the qualified level is fully equivalent to a handwritten signature. Its implementing decree (Feb 2025) names DGSSI as the national authority, and the law lays the legal base for a national digital-identity system. No reliable published data was found on language use in insurance interactions.

### Cited Findings
- WhatsApp is used by 75% of Moroccans, and 95% of users connect daily. 81% of Moroccans are on social media and 19% are offline (Sunergia barometer, phone survey of 1,009 people, Jan–Feb 2025, ±3%) — [TelQuel](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2025/05/30/reseaux-sociaux-au-maroc-whatsapp-facebook-et-instagram-dominent-19-des-marocains-toujours-hors-ligne_1935598/); [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/06/206465/2025-social-media-barometer-81-of-moroccans-connected-digital-divide-persists/); [Hespress EN](https://en.hespress.com/112276-112276.html)
- Among 18–24-year-olds, 88% use WhatsApp (same barometer) — [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/06/206465/2025-social-media-barometer-81-of-moroccans-connected-digital-divide-persists/)
- Mobile subscriptions reached 59.16 m at end-Dec 2025, and internet penetration was 112.59% (subscriptions, not unique users) — [AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202603180488.html); [Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2025/08/256085/moroccos-mobile-subscribers-reach-58-8-million-in-first-half-of-2025/)
- Law 43-20 on trust services for electronic transactions was adopted unanimously in late 2020. The Arabic text was published in the Bulletin Officiel on 11 Jan 2021, with the French version reported on 18 March 2021. It repealed the e-signature provisions of Law 53-05 — [Aujourd'hui le Maroc](https://aujourdhui.ma/economie/e-transactions-une-loi-pour-restaurer-la-confiance); [FNH](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/un-nouveau-cadre-juridique-pour-les-transactions-electroniques)
- Under Law 43-20 there are three e-signature levels: simple, advanced and qualified. Only the qualified signature has full equivalence with a handwritten one and a legal presumption of reliability. Accredited providers verify signer identity before issuing certificates — [SNRT News](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/signature-en-ligne-au-maroc-entre-securite-technique-absolue-et-parfaite-equivalence-legale); [MarocLaw (Arabic)](https://maroclaw.com/?p=18574)
- The implementing decree, published on the Adala portal in Feb 2025, designates DGSSI (Direction générale de la sécurité des systèmes d'information) as the national authority — [Adala decree PDF](https://adala.justice.gov.ma/api/uploads/2025/02/24/D%C3%A9cret%20%20confiance%20pour%20les%20transactions%20%C3%A9lectroniques-1740412902700.pdf); [H24info](https://h24info.ma/?p=453153)
- The law sets the legal and functional framework for a national digital-identity system — [FNH](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/transaction-numerique-un-pas-de-plus-vers-la-signature-electronique)
- An electronic contract has the same evidentiary weight as paper if both parties are duly identified. E-signature requires a reliable identification process attested by a certificate of conformity (ACAPS online-sales context) — [Médias24 2023](https://medias24.com/2023/06/06/assurances-la-vente-en-ligne-a-la-traine-malgre-un-cadre-reglementaire-complet/); [ACAPS newsletter 8](https://www.acaps.ma/sites/default/files/publication_documents/acaps_newsletter8_vf.pdf)

### Inferences
- For front-office subscription, which is face-to-face or assisted, a simple or advanced e-signature plus the agent's identity check may be enough for low-value travel contracts. Fully remote sales would lean on qualified signatures or certified identity proofing, which makes them heavier. This is a legal inference and should be checked against the ACAPS instruction text.
- The tool's UI and AI outputs should be French-first, with Arabic support and probably Darija for conversational assistance. This is industry common sense, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No published data was found on the language mix (French, MSA, Darija, Amazigh) in Moroccan insurance customer interactions. 2024 census language statistics were not retrieved.
- No source was retrieved on the CNIE (electronic ID card) chip, the DGSN digital-identity or "e-identification" service, or how insurers might use it for KYC. The link between CNIE and DGSN is unconfirmed in this pass.
- The remote-KYC requirements in the ACAPS online-sales instruction were not retrieved.
- No assessment of Moroccan data protection (Law 09-08, CNDP) constraints on AI and data transfer was done in this pass.
